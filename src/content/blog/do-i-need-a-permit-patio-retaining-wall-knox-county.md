One of the most common questions we get before a project: **do I need a permit?** Rules vary by
jurisdiction and change over time, so always confirm with your local building department — but here's
the general lay of the land in the Knoxville / Knox County area, and how we handle it.

> This is general guidance, not official code. Requirements differ between the City of Knoxville,
> Knox County, and towns like Farragut, and they change. We verify and pull the right permits for
> your specific project.

## Retaining walls

**Often yes.** Retaining walls over a certain height — commonly **4 feet** measured from the bottom
of the footing — typically require an **engineered design and a permit.** Shorter garden and seat
walls usually don't. Tiered walls can trigger requirements too, depending on spacing. This is safety,
not red tape: a tall wall holds back tons of soil.

## Patios (on grade)

**Usually no** for a standard at-grade concrete or paver patio, since it's not a structure. But
HOAs (common in Farragut and newer subdivisions) may have their own approval process, and drainage
changes near property lines can matter.

## Driveways

**Sometimes.** A new or widened driveway that ties into a public road often needs a **right-of-way or
driveway permit** from the city/county or TDOT, especially for the apron. Replacing in the same
footprint is usually simpler.

## Gas fire features

**Often yes** for the gas work involved in a gas fire pit or fire feature — gas lines are permitted
and inspected.
