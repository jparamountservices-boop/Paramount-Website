It's the most common question we get on patios and pool decks: **stamped concrete or pavers?** Since
we install both, here's the honest comparison — no sales pitch for whichever one has a better margin.

## The 30-second version

- **Stamped concrete** = lower cost, seamless look, but a crack shows across the whole slab.
- **Pavers** = higher cost, flex with the ground, repairable one unit at a time, last longer.

Both look great. The right choice comes down to budget, how long you plan to stay, and how our soil
behaves under your specific yard.

## Cost

Stamped concrete is usually the more affordable option:

- **Stamped concrete:** upwards of ~$18–$20 per square foot
- **Pavers:** ~$25–$35 per square foot (patios), more for driveways

Pavers cost more because every unit is set by hand on a deep, compacted base — it's far more labor.

## Durability in our climate

This is where East Tennessee matters. Our clay soil holds water and moves with freeze-thaw cycles.

- **Stamped concrete** is a single rigid slab. Built right — proper base, reinforcement, control
  joints — it holds up well, but any ground movement can eventually cause a crack, and that crack is
  permanent.
- **Pavers** are flexible by design. They move *with* the ground instead of fighting it, which is why
  a good paver installation can last 50+ years without cracking.

## Repairs

- **Stamped concrete:** a stain or crack means patching or resurfacing a section — and matching color
  on old concrete is tough.
- **Pavers:** lift the damaged unit, reset it, done. You can even keep a few spares from the original
  batch.

## Looks & maintenance

Stamped concrete gives you a **seamless** surface in stone, slate, brick or wood-plank patterns, and
should be resealed every couple of years to keep color rich. Pavers offer a huge range of colors,
shapes and patterns, need their joints re-sanded occasionally, and can grow weeds if they weren't
installed with polymeric sand and edge restraint (ours are).

## So which should you choose?

- Choose **stamped concrete** if you want a high-end look at a lower price and a seamless surface.
- Choose **pavers** if you want maximum longevity, easy repairs, and you're building a forever-home
  outdoor space.

Either way, the install quality matters more than the material. A cheap paver job on a bad base fails
faster than good stamped concrete, and vice versa.
