Building a deck in East Tennessee means one early decision: **pressure-treated wood or composite
(like Trex)?** Our humidity, sun and rain make this matter more than it does in drier climates.

## Cost

- **Pressure-treated wood:** lower up-front cost — the budget-friendly build.
- **Composite:** costs more to install, but the price gap narrows over the deck's life once you factor in maintenance.

## Maintenance — the real difference

This is where East Tennessee's climate decides it for many homeowners.

- **Wood** needs regular upkeep: cleaning, and staining or sealing every 1–3 years. Skip it in our humidity and boards gray, crack and rot faster.
- **Composite** needs essentially none — an occasional wash. No staining, no sealing, ever.

## Lifespan

- **Wood:** ~10–15 years with good maintenance; less without.
- **Composite:** 25–30+ years, and most brands carry long warranties (often 25 years).

## Heat & feel

Composite can run warmer underfoot in direct summer sun — lighter colors and shaded designs help. Wood stays a touch cooler but demands the upkeep above. Both feel solid when built on proper framing.

## Looks

Wood has natural grain and can be stained any color (until it needs redoing). Modern composite mimics wood grain convincingly and holds its color for decades without fading.

## Bottom line

- Choose **wood** if up-front budget is tight and you don't mind regular maintenance.
- Choose **composite** if you want to build it once and basically forget it — the popular choice for East Tennessee's humidity.
