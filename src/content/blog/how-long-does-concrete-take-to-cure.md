One of the most common questions we get after a pour is simple: **"When can I use it?"** New concrete
sets up enough to look finished within a day, but it keeps *curing* — chemically hardening and gaining
strength — for weeks. Rushing it is how you get early cracks, scuffs and scaling. Here's the honest
timeline for a Knoxville-area project.

## The quick timeline

- **24–48 hours:** you can usually walk on it carefully. Keep pets and kids off.
- **~7 days:** it's reached most of its early strength — fine for foot traffic and patio furniture.
- **28–30 days:** fully cured and safe to park vehicles on a new driveway.
- **~28 days before sealing:** concrete needs to release moisture before a sealer goes on.

## Why "set" and "cured" aren't the same

Concrete **sets** (goes from wet to firm) in a few hours. It **cures** — gains its real strength — over
about 28 days, and technically keeps hardening for months. It gains roughly 70% of its strength in the
first week and most of the rest over the following three weeks. That's why we ask you to wait on
vehicles even when the surface feels rock-hard after a day or two.

## What affects curing time in East Tennessee

Our weather genuinely changes the timeline:

- **Temperature.** Concrete cures slower in cold weather and faster in heat. Our cold snaps can stretch
  the timeline; blazing summer days can make it cure *too* fast without proper moisture control.
- **Humidity & rain.** Fresh concrete actually wants to stay damp as it cures — moisture is part of the
  chemical reaction. A sudden downpour right after finishing, though, can mar the surface.
- **Slab thickness.** A thick driveway or load-bearing slab takes longer to reach full strength than a
  thin walkway.
- **The mix.** Higher-PSI mixes and additives change how fast it gains strength.

## How we protect a fresh pour

Good curing isn't passive — it's part of doing the job right:

- We time pours around the weather when we can, avoiding hard freezes and downpours.
- We finish at the right moment (too early or too late both cause problems).
- We cut or tool control joints so the inevitable shrinkage happens in a straight line, not a random crack.
- In extreme heat we keep the surface from drying out too fast so it doesn't craze or dust.

## Bottom line

**Walk on it after a day or two, use a patio after about a week, and wait a full month before parking a
car on a new driveway.** Give it that time and the concrete rewards you with decades of service. Rush
it and you can undo good work in an afternoon.
